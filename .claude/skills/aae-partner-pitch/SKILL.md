# Partner pitch

Partners are the fastest source of repeat customers: a mover, realtor or
apartment office meets people at the exact moment they need furniture
assembled and TVs mounted. A partner reads a pitch in seconds and decides
whether we are a serious business they can trust in their client's home. So the
pitch is specific to their business, uses real prices, and promises nothing we
cannot do.

## 1. Gather the facts first

- **Prices**: read them from `assets/js/booking-source-of-truth.js` (items, and
  `bundles` such as `move-in-ready`). Quote "from" prices for the 3 to 5 services
  that matter to this partner type. Never round or invent a price.
- **Reviews**: say "5-star Google reviews" only after checking the current rating
  and count (the owner can read them from the Business Profile); otherwise leave
  reviews out.
- **Coverage**: customers can book across Texas; pros are confirmed for each
  appointment. Say "across Austin, Houston, San Antonio and Dallas-Fort Worth and
  wider Texas". Never claim a local office, a crew size, or same-day availability
  in a specific city.
- **Their referral link**: every partner has a personal link in the owner
  dashboard (Partners panel) carrying their code, so their bookings are counted
  and credited. Tell the owner to copy it from there and leave a clear
  placeholder `[your partner link from the Partners panel]` in the draft. Do not
  build the link by hand.

## 2. What the pitch must get right, and why

- **Tools only.** Our pros bring their own tools. Customers supply their own
  furniture, mounts and hardware. Never say we provide mounts, hardware or parts.
- **No invented offers.** No discounts, commissions or referral fees unless the
  owner states one in the request. Partners hold you to whatever you write.
- **No unverified claims**: no "insured", "licensed", "background-checked",
  "bonded", "guaranteed same-day". Things that are true: pros are independent
  and identity-verified before they receive jobs; the customer pays nothing until
  the job is done; flat, upfront prices; booking online takes a few minutes; a
  reassembly guarantee on our workmanship (see `terms.html` section 9 for its
  exact scope before describing it).
- **Business voice**: "we", "AssembleAtEase". Sign as "The AssembleAtEase Team",
  never the founder's name. Legal name in documents: AssembleAtEase LLC.
  Contact: (979) 232-5139, service@assembleatease.com.
- **No emojis**, no hype words ("revolutionary", "best in Texas").

## 3. Angle by partner type

| Partner | What they care about | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Mover | Clients who need beds, TVs and furniture put back up | Move-in Ready bundle; booked for the day after the move |
| Realtor | A thoughtful closing gift, clients settled fast | Move-in setup they can give or recommend |
| Property manager / apartment office | Move-ins, staging, fewer residents drilling into walls | TV mounting and furniture assembly for move-ins; one contact |
| Furniture store | Buyers who won't assemble | Assembly from the price list; their customers book directly |
| Office furnisher | Desks, chairs, workstations | Office assembly prices; scheduling around business hours |

## 4. Formats

- **Intro email** (default): subject line, then 90 to 140 words: who we are in
  one sentence, why it helps *their* clients, 3 real prices, the partner link,
  a soft next step ("Would it help if we sent a few cards for your front desk?").
- **Text / DM**: 2 to 3 sentences plus the link.
- **Follow-up**: 50 to 80 words, new useful detail, no guilt ("just checking in").
- **One-page leave-behind**: headline, three benefits for their clients, a short
  price list, how booking works (3 steps), contact and link. If the owner wants a
  printable file, write it as a Word or PDF document with the document skills.

## 5. Hand it over

Give the draft ready to paste, then one line listing every price and claim used
and where it came from, so the owner can check nothing was invented. The owner
sends it; never send messages yourself.
