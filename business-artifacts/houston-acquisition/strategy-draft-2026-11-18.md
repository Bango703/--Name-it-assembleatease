> - Bidding: keep Maximize clicks with the $4 cap; the Manual CPC $7/$5/$4 proposal is withdrawn (single-job economics, plan section 6).

AssembleAtEase should run Houston Google Search as a capped measurement test of about $300 a month, led by TV mounting. Any larger budget stays unearned until three things are true: Google can see and correctly value real bookings, the one Houston Easer's service ZIPs and weekly open slots are written down, and the business has measured what share of paid clicks become completed, captured jobs. Demand is not the constraint. Keyword Planner shows **TV mounting search volume in Houston is roughly ten times furniture assembly** (BUSINESS DATA). The constraints are measurement, supply and margin. The account has recorded **one conversion in $380.08 of all-time spend**, its primary conversion action is flagged misconfigured, it records only after a cookie Accept, the value it sends includes sales tax, and **one Easer carries all Houston fulfillment** (BUSINESS DATA, first_party_data.md §3-4). The margin is thin. A $119 TV job contributes about **$31.66** after card fees, and completed small furniture jobs contributed **$27-$38**. The top-of-page bid estimates for the highest-intent Houston TV terms start at **$5.90-$7.53**. At a $4 average cost per click, a base TV job breaks even only if about **one paid click in eight** becomes a completed job; at $6, one in five (HYPOTHESIS arithmetic). Bigger carts change the picture: a 65-inch over-fireplace mount ($324) or a playset ($346-$695) breaks even at about one completed job per 22 to 47 clicks at $4. The plan has six moves. Fund TV mounting first and bid to reach top-of-page on high-intent terms with Manual CPC. Retarget to the Easer's real ZIPs: two of the three Houston-area customers were in Spring and Katy, outside the current City of Houston target. Pause the small-ticket furniture ad group. Build outdoor and fitness ad groups now and switch them on in their seasons. Fix the /book item step, where only **7 of 40 measured visitors** finished choosing items. Meta is unavailable, so retargeting runs through Google lists, follow-up email and partners. Spending above $300 a month waits for a second Houston Easer and for a measured cost per completed paid booking at or below first-job contribution.

Every conclusion below carries one of four labels. **VERIFIED** means a cited source states it. **BUSINESS DATA** means it comes from AssembleAtEase's own database, ad account, GA4 or code (first_party_data.md). **HYPOTHESIS** means it is reasoned or modeled and not proven. **TEST REQUIRED** means only the business's own data can settle it. External benchmarks are labeled as external comparators, with their source, date and sample, and a note on why they may not fit this business.

## 1. Houston's binding constraints are measurement and one Easer, not search demand

Houston has demand. The metro reached **7,904,627 people on July 1, 2025**, the largest numeric gain of any US metro that year (+126,720) (VERIFIED, [Census](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2025-popest-metro-micro-counties.html)). It holds about **1.09 million renter households** (VERIFIED, [Census Reporter ACS 2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B25003,B19013,B25119&geo_ids=31000US26420,31000US19100,31000US12420,31000US41700,16000US4835000,16000US4805000,16000US4819000,16000US4865000)). It delivered **16,210 apartment units in the 12 months to Q2 2026**, against 9,307 absorbed, so new-lease move-ins happen every month (VERIFIED, [MMG](https://mmgrea.com/houston-q2-2026-market-report/)). In Keyword Planner, "tv mounting" alone draws 1K-10K searches a month in the City of Houston, and "tv mounting houston", "near me" and "service" draw 100-1K each. Every furniture-assembly term sits at 10-100 in the city (BUSINESS DATA, first_party_data.md §5). The landing page is fast: mobile PageSpeed Performance 96 and LCP 2.0 s on the TV landing URL (BUSINESS DATA, §7). AssembleAtEase's TV prices are at market with Houston flat-rate specialists, as section 7 shows.

What blocks profitable growth is mostly self-inflicted and fixable. **First, Google is close to blind.** The "Book appointment" primary conversion last recorded on May 18, 2026. "Add to cart (1)" is also set as a primary action and counts every add. The Google tag records an ads conversion only after the visitor clicks Accept. The conversion value sent is the reservation total including 8.25% tax, not platform revenue. No booking stores a Google click ID, so offline import is impossible today (BUSINESS DATA, §4). The one reliable path is newer: since PR #467 on 2026-11-18, tagged /book links store their UTM source on the booking without consent. The campaign's URL suffix carries `utm_campaign=houston_search` (BUSINESS DATA, §2, §4). That database count, not the Google Ads conversion column, has to be the decision metric until tracking is fixed.

**Second, supply is one person.** Seven Easers are approved, active and available statewide. Exactly one is in the Houston area, in ZIP 770, with 4 completed jobs. One more Houston-area applicant is pending in ZIP 775. His service ZIPs, weekly slots and days off were not in the data pull (BUSINESS DATA, §3). Every paid click that converts on a day he cannot work becomes a stranded booking, which CLAUDE.md classes as P0.

**Third, margin on the highest-volume intents is thin.** Section 8 shows the numbers. **Zero of nine** completed customers have booked again, so no repeat revenue offsets acquisition cost yet (BUSINESS DATA, §2).

**Fourth, targeting misses where Houston customers actually were.** The campaign targets the City of Houston. The three Houston-metro bookings came from Spring (ZIP 773, twice) and Katy (ZIP 774). Google treats both as separate location targets (BUSINESS DATA, §2).

The account history points the same way. A Smart campaign bought 181 clicks at about $0.58 and produced no conversions. The Austin Search campaign's only conversion came from "furniture assembly near me", which averaged **$11.23 per click** over 3 clicks (BUSINESS DATA, §4). One conversion proves nothing on its own. It is still a warning that cheap clicks have not been cheap bookings.

The board's position follows from those facts. The VP of Finance protects cash, the COO protects capacity, and the Head of Data needs a working measurement chain, so Houston paid search is a 90-day, capacity-gated learning budget, not a growth engine. The fact that would change this is a measured trailing-60-day cost per completed paid booking at or below first-job contribution, on at least five completed paid jobs.

## 3. The 07:00-22:00 schedule with afternoon weighting is a test, not a finding

An earlier statement to the owner was wrong, and it is corrected here plainly: **Maximize clicks does apply ad-schedule bid adjustments.** Google states that "Ad scheduling bid adjustments are available for campaigns using the Maximize clicks automated bid strategy" and that adjustments "apply on top of the maximum CPC bid limit you've set" (VERIFIED, [Google 6372644](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372644?hl=en); [Google 6268626](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268626)). Manual CPC accepts them as well. Smart Bidding strategies (Maximize conversions, Target CPA, Target ROAS) behave differently. They already use "local day of week and time of day" as auction-time signals, they ignore manual bid adjustments, and they still obey the schedule's on and off hours (VERIFIED, [Google 7065882](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882); [Google 2732132](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2732132)). Under Smart Bidding, the schedule becomes a pure on/off switch.

Two timing effects distort any schedule decision. **Click-date reporting** credits a conversion to the day of the click, not the day of the booking, so the latest one to two weeks always look under-converted. Google offers "Conversions (by conv. time)" columns for this (VERIFIED, [Google 9549009](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9549009?hl=en)). **Capped budgets may spend early.** In a 20-account sample of lead-generation advertisers (Jul-Sep 2026), the 12 accounts that lost impression share to budget put 60% of spend into hours 7-11, against 43.5% for the rest. This is an external comparator, the vertical mix is undisclosed, and the cause is the author's own read ([Conner Crowe](https://connercrowe.com/benchmarks/google-ads-account-benchmarks/)). If that pattern holds at $10 a day, the budget runs out before the afternoon hours in which 74% of past bookings were placed. Narrowing the hours does not save money either, because campaigns pace toward 30.4 times the daily budget each month (VERIFIED, [Google 2404244](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404244)).

The recommendation is to keep the 07:00-22:00 window and weight it, then check it on day 14.

## 5. Run one Houston Search campaign, bid to top-of-page on high-intent TV terms, and pool all data

## 6. Without Meta, retargeting runs through Google lists, follow-up messages and partners

Meta (Facebook and Instagram) advertising is not available to this business: the owner's profile has been restricted from advertising since 2020 with no appeal path, and the owner has excluded TikTok. This plan therefore contains no Meta campaign (BUSINESS DATA, §1).

The realistic alternatives are smaller and slower, and each needs its own gate. **Google remarketing lists for Search (RLSA)** are the cheapest retargeting option and stay inside the Google-only rule. Lists now need only **100 active users in 30 days** on Search (VERIFIED, [Google 7476585](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7476585)). The site saw only 81 consented GA4 users in four weeks (BUSINESS DATA, §6), and remarketing depends on consent, so the list will fill slowly. **Primary versus secondary.** Primary actions feed bidding and secondary actions are "for observation only" (VERIFIED, [Google 11461796](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11461796)). The primary should be **"Booking placed, card authorized"**. It fires within minutes, it is the deepest real-intent event with any volume, and Google advises bidding on the stage "closest to a sale" that has about 15 conversions in 30 days (VERIFIED, [Google 13775965](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13775965?hl=en)). "Add to cart (1)" must be demoted to secondary today. As a primary that "counts every", it teaches Google that browsing is the goal (BUSINESS DATA, §4; HYPOTHESIS on effect). Never make the booking tag and the completed-job upload primary at the same time, because that counts one customer twice (HYPOTHESIS, google_ads_mechanics_and_tracking.md §3). GA4 imports stay secondary, which Google itself sets to avoid double counting (VERIFIED, [Google 10632359](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10632359)).

**Dedup and cleanup.** Google "may only process the first instance of a given Transaction ID" across tag and upload, so the booking ref must be identical everywhere. It must contain no personal data and stay within 64 characters (VERIFIED, [Google 6386790](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6386790)). Cancelled bookings get a RETRACT by order ID. Upload it at least 24 hours after the original, within 7 days so bidding sees it, and in all cases within 54 days. Completed jobs at a different amount get a RESTATE (VERIFIED, [Google 7686280](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7686280)). New offline imports must use the **Data Manager API** (VERIFIED, [Google Ads API](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/conversions/upload-clicks)).

**The value fix.** Send platform contribution computed by the server, not the tax-inclusive reservation total. Tax is pass-through, and the current value overstates return about four times (section 8). This touches book.html next to the payment path, so it needs audit-before-code and a three-role test (BUSINESS DATA on current behavior; HYPOTHESIS on the fix).

**The consent decision.** Google's EU consent policy covers the EEA, UK and Switzerland (VERIFIED, [Google 16724512](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16724512?hl=en)). Texas obligations, including the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, were not researched. The question already sits with General Counsel as backlog item BOOKING-SOURCE-COVERAGE. The board asks for a ruling within 30 days on two points: the default consent state for US visitors, and storing click IDs on bookings. Every offline import, retraction and completed-job signal depends on that answer. Until then, the database UTM count decides, and the Google column is a partial view (HYPOTHESIS). **What to optimize toward:** nothing automated for now, because Manual CPC does not bid on conversions. At 15 or more net primary conversions in 30 days, test Maximize conversions. Promote "completed and captured" to primary only when it alone reaches about 15 in 30 days (HYPOTHESIS, grounded in [Google 13775965](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13775965?hl=en)).

**The funnel leak comes before any of this.** Only **7 of 40 measured /book visitors (17.5%)** finished choosing items. After that step, 3-5 of 7 reached a booking or quote (BUSINESS DATA, §6). The TV landing screen opens straight onto the size list. Nothing on that first screen says when an Easer can come, that the card is charged after the job, or what the total will include (BUSINESS DATA, §7, an observation, not a tested defect). Three changes, all true statements, are recommended. Add one line above the list stating facts that are already true. Put "plus $5 service call and tax" beside the from-prices. Instrument the step, with events for size selected and add-on viewed, to show where people quit. Judge the change on item-step completion, the highest-volume step, by comparing 4 weeks before with 4 weeks after (HYPOTHESIS; TEST REQUIRED). If paid item completion stays below 20% after 8 weeks, test the Houston TV page as the landing page instead.

## 11. Capacity gates the ads, and recruitment runs ahead of any budget above $300 a month

Operator evidence supports constraining a marketplace before scaling it. Sixteen of 17 marketplaces Lenny Rachitsky studied constrained themselves early by geography or category, and about 14 went after supply first (secondary counts, [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)). Labor marketplaces are typically supply-constrained (VERIFIED, [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-know-if-youre-supply-or-demand)). No published case was found of a marketplace throttling its own paid ads by supply availability (VERIFIED absence), so AssembleAtEase writes its own rules. With one Houston Easer, Houston is supply-constrained by definition. A paid booking he cannot take costs the customer a cancellation, wastes the click, and breaks Rule 16.

The safeguards work in layers. **Geography** matches his served ZIPs. **The calendar** is the real gate: confirm that /book cannot accept a Houston appointment on a date he has not opened (TEST REQUIRED). The data does not show whether it can. **Budget sizing** keeps peak-case weekly paid bookings at or below his confirmed open slots (section 9). **A Monday capacity check** pauses the campaign the same day if Houston open slots for the next 7 days drop below 2. **The fill-rate metric** is the share of paid Houston bookings an Easer accepts within 2 business hours of the offer and completes on the requested date. It adapts Thumbtack's liquidity test of "60% of searches get 3+ quotes" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-know-if-youre-supply-or-demand)) and should stay above 90%. Below that, ads pause and recruitment starts (HYPOTHESIS thresholds). **A storm rule** pauses ads on a named-storm watch or landfall in the Houston area. After Hurricane Beryl, local trades were tied up for weeks (VERIFIED anecdotal, [Fox 26](https://www.fox26houston.com/news/rebuilding-after-beryl-homeowners-face-long-wait-fence-repairs)).

**Recruitment runs ahead of customer ads** in four situations:

- Before any move above $300 a month, which requires a second approved Houston Easer as backup.
- When the Easer's utilization stays above 60% for two weeks.
- When any paid booking is declined or cancelled for lack of an Easer.
- Six to eight weeks before a seasonal peak, which allows time for identity verification, the agreement and owner approval.

That last situation makes the pending ZIP 775 applicant urgent now, ahead of the December-February TV season. Job boards supplied about 50% of one labor marketplace's early supply. Referral bonuses paid on activation ($50 per activated pro at Pared) are a documented pattern (VERIFIED, [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/labor-marketplace-supply-growth)). Indeed sponsored jobs bill per click or per started application, with conflicting minimums (VERIFIED, [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/how-pricing-works-on-indeed)). Under the Google-only rule, any paid job-board test needs the owner's approval. Measure cost per *activated* Easer (approved, with a first completed job), never cost per applicant. Recruitment copy should lead with per-job pay and payout speed. Vendor-sponsored surveys rank fast payouts second only to pay level ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/disbursements/2025/instant-payouts-turn-one-time-users-into-lifelong-loyalists/)). It must also read as an independent business opportunity: choose your jobs, set your availability, bring your own tools, work other platforms. That language follows the IRS control factors and the Texas Workforce Commission's nine-criteria marketplace test (VERIFIED, [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee); [40 TAC 815.134](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/40-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-815-134)). Avoid "hiring", "shifts" and "wage". GA4 shows 5 Easer applications started and 4 submitted in four weeks (BUSINESS DATA, §6), so the organic funnel works. It just isn't aimed at Houston.

## 15. Pre-committed rules for testing, scaling, pausing and reallocating

These rules are written before the first click so that no single good or bad week drives a decision. The thresholds are HYPOTHESIS unless marked. They rest on Google's evaluation guidance, the economics in section 8, and binomial arithmetic. One booking in 40 clicks has an exact 95% interval of **0.06% to 13%**, which cannot separate a bad campaign from a good one (HYPOTHESIS, standard statistics, checked).

| Type | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Testing | One change at a time; hold each for at least 14 days or 2 conversion cycles, whichever is longer | VERIFIED guidance, [Google 13020501](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501) |
| Testing | Judge page changes on item-step completion, ad changes on CTR plus item completion, and spend on cost per completed paid booking after a 21-day lag | Volume exists only at the top of the funnel; median booking lead time is 2 days, up to 21 (BUSINESS DATA) |
| Testing | No booking-rate A/B tests until roughly 100 conversions per variant; use before-and-after with intervals instead | External practitioner comparator, [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/google-ads-experiments-b2b-saas-statistical-significance-methodology) |
| Pause (stop-loss) | **$300 spent with zero authorized paid bookings: pause and audit** search terms, landing page and tracking | At a 3% booking rate, 75 clicks produce zero bookings only about 10% of the time |
| Pause (stop-loss) | **$600 spent with zero completed paid jobs: pause** and rethink the service mix | At a 2.5% completed rate, 150 clicks produce zero only about 2% of the time |
| Pause (capacity) | Open Houston slots for the next 7 days below 2, or any paid booking declined or cancelled for lack of an Easer: pause the same day | A stranded booking is P0 (CLAUDE.md) |
| Pause (external) | Named-storm watch or landfall in the Houston area; overdue account balance; a test booking that loses its UTM | Operational safety; measurement integrity |
| Pause (unit loss) | With 5 or more completed paid jobs, trailing-60-day cost per completed paid booking above average contribution: drop a tier or pause the weakest ad group | Ceiling = break-even (section 8) |
| Scale | All must hold: 5 or more completed paid jobs; trailing-60-day cost per completed paid booking at or below **80%** of average contribution; lost IS (budget) at least 20%; tracking coverage at least 80%; capacity gate from section 9 met | Buys only demand that exists, is measured and can be staffed |
| Scale | Move up one tier at a time ($300, $750, $1,500, $3,000) and hold each for at least 4 weeks | Budget changes do not reset learning (VERIFIED, [Google 13020501](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501)); conversion lag does |
| Bid strategy | 15 or more net primary conversions in 30 days: test Maximize conversions with no target and remove schedule adjustments; 30 or more: consider Target CPA, set near the measured cost per completed booking | VERIFIED thresholds, [Google 13775965](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13775965?hl=en); [Google 6268632](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) |
| Reallocate (ad group) | Compare ad groups on cost per completed paid booking; declare a loser only after $300 spent or 3 completed jobs in that ad group | Avoids judging on noise |
| Reallocate (season) | Outdoor on Feb 15-May 15 and Oct 1-Nov 30; Fitness on Dec 26-Feb 28; TV year-round, weighted Nov 27-Feb 14 | Section 12 |
| Reallocate (channel) | A new channel (Microsoft, Nextdoor, LSA) needs owner approval, takes at most 20% of paid budget, and is judged on the same completed-job metric over at least 60 days | One metric across channels |
| Reallocate (city) | No second paid city until Houston has 10 or more completed paid jobs at or below the ceiling and the owner lifts the Houston-only rule; Dallas-Fort Worth first | Completion before expansion |

## Conclusion

The Chairman's ruling: run Houston as a $300-a-month, TV-led, capacity-gated test for 90 days. Enable it only after the Easer's ZIPs and slots are written down, the primary conversion is cleaned up, and a tagged test booking proves the database can count paid jobs. Accept a capped learning loss of up to about $600-$900 in exchange for the one number nobody has: the share of paid Houston clicks that become completed, captured jobs. Two things were missed by framing this as an advertising question. The first is that the business has been running a measurement system that values a $119 job as if tax were revenue and only "sees" visitors who click Accept. Every bid decision built on that would have been wrong, however good the ads were. The second is that the profitable lane is not the high-volume one. TV mounting is where the searches are, but outdoor and fitness work, along with large TV jobs with add-ons, is where a paid click can pay for itself. The plan uses TV mounting to learn and the seasonal ad groups to earn.

What changes going forward is the order of work. Supply and measurement come before spend: the pending Houston applicant and the General Counsel consent ruling matter more to growth in the next 60 days than any keyword. The fastest gain available is not a bid change. It is lifting item-step completion on /book above 17.5%, because every later step converts at roughly half or better (3-5 of 7). If the $300 test returns a cost per completed paid booking near the high case ($54-$80), Houston paid search becomes a scalable engine behind a second and third Easer. If it returns the mid case ($160-$240), the money moves to partners, larger carts and the seasonal ad groups, and the business has learned that cheaply rather than at $3,000 a month.
