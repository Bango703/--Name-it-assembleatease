# AssembleAtEase organic search and booking audit

Reviewed October 7, 2026. Scope: unpaid search discovery, Google Business Profile, customer landing pages, acquisition measurement, and the conversion of demand into completed work. Evidence comes from the open production accounts and public website. This is not a whole-platform security, legal, payout, or launch certification.

## 1. Executive Summary

**AssembleAtEase's customer service pages receive very little organic traffic in the major Texas hubs. Broad indexing is already healthy; incomplete measurement and documented fulfillment difficulties are additional problems.** These findings support a focused organic growth program, but do not prove a single cause for every lost booking.

- Search Console reports **402 of 412 sitemap URLs indexed (97.6%)**, as of October 3. None of the six service pages for Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Dallas, or Tyler is among the ten sitemap exclusions.
- Each city's six service pages received only **26 clicks for Austin, 5 for San Antonio, 3 for Houston, 4 for Dallas, and 0 for Tyler** from July 5 through October 4. These are landing-page groups, not all searches or customers physically located in those cities.
- Google Business Profile produced **5 call actions and 2 website clicks** during July–September. The inspected profile covers Austin/Central Texas. It is not evidence of strong Maps visibility across the state.
- GA4 recorded **284 Organic Search sessions, 36 active users, and 31 new users** during July 9–October 6. Returning visits, recruitment activity, and possible internal/test traffic limit what the session total says about prospective customers.
- Two Tyler bookings from the same customer ended canceled after documented staffing and scheduling changes. Neither the customer-stated cause nor competitors' coverage was established.
- Six recorded completed jobs reconcile to **$1,846.16 in successful Stripe payments**. Their original acquisition sources and the real/test classification of older work remain incompletely verified.

**Recommendation:** protect accepted work, establish a truthful organic-to-completed-job baseline, and improve existing customer pages with genuine local proof and relevant earned links. Prioritize Austin; support San Antonio and Houston with verified completed work and reliable capacity. Expand effort in other markets when service scope, travel, and appointment coverage are dependable.

## 4. P1 Issues

### P1.1: Customer search reach is too small for dependable volume

The main city pages exist and most are indexed. Their observed click counts are low. GSC's external-links report lists three links, all to the homepage, from BBB, MapQuest, and Yahoo. This is Google's reported sample, not a complete count of every link on the web.

Five inspected furniture pages reuse the same dresser/bed photos. Austin labels its example as an Austin project; Houston, San Antonio, Dallas and Tyler use honest generic example labels, with no observed local completed-job case study or city-specific testimonial. Their scope/preparation content is meaningfully different, so they are not simply empty city-name copies.

**Recommended action:** SEO / Organic Growth Engineer and VP of Marketing should strengthen the existing Austin furniture page and its relevant guides using query intent, useful project detail, and verified permissioned local work. Use the actual San Antonio furniture and Houston/Spring outdoor jobs to support the corresponding service pages where scope and permission match. Earn relevant links/citations through real local relationships. Do not invent project locations, reviews, or unsupported availability.

### P1.2: Organic tracking cannot yet establish reliable booking conversion

| Metric | Observed |
| --- | ---: |
| Sessions / active users / new users | 284 / 36 / 31 |
| Google organic / Bing organic sessions | 203 / 53 |

| `/book` landing sessions / active users | 30 / 7 |
| Recruitment/application landing sessions | 19 |
| Begin-checkout events / total users | 21 / 9 |
| `booking_completed` events / total users | 4 / 3 |
| Key events / recorded GA4 revenue | 0 / $0 |

`booking_completed` is browser confirmation, including quote/future-booking paths; it is not proof of completed work or captured payment. Current key-event definitions were not independently inspected, so zero summary results do not alone prove a particular configuration is disabled. Aggregate step counts are not a sequenced funnel: some completion counts exceed start counts. Do not calculate exact abandonment from them.

GA4's Organic Search channel also includes 22 `ntp.msn.com / referral` sessions. A malformed landing path contained pasted assistant/deployment-log text. These observations warrant a deployed tracking/internal-traffic review; they do not establish that all repeated visits are bots or owner traffic.

Recent **September 9–October 6 versus August 12–September 8**: sessions fell **117 to 47**, but new users increased **12 to 13**. This is not a demonstrated 60% fall in newly acquired people. GA4 dates differ from GSC; these are separate signals, not matched-period reconciliation.

Owner has 20 stored booking records, seven explicitly flagged tests. The remaining 13 contain six completed, six canceled and one accepted. Five source entries are self-referrals; others are Google 3, owner_manual 2, direct 1, DuckDuckGo 1, online 1. These include cancellations and do not reliably recover original acquisition. The three unbooked demand requests include two closed apparent-spam entries and one apparent owner test. Demand totals are not a clean pipeline.

**Recommended action:** Head of Data & AI and Data Analyst should reconcile each real inquiry/booking with its service city, first known source/landing page, outcome, cancellation reason, payment, and explicit test classification. Keep lead submission, accepted booking, completed work, and captured payment distinct. Review deployed event behavior and existing first-source storage before proposing changes; do not collect customer PII in GA4. Owner classification of the older May/July completed records remains pending.

### P1.3: The inspected Business Profile is a Central Texas presence

July–September GBP: **221 views, seven interactions, five call actions, two website clicks, zero GBP bookings**. Calls are button actions, not verified answered/qualified calls. GBP booking zero does not measure every website booking.

The profile has **5.0 stars from 13 reviews**, all with owner responses, and recent posts. Three newest reviews are about five–six weeks old; nine of the thirteen date to May/June 2024. Lack of posting is not an established problem. Thirteen listed service areas are Austin/Central Texas; Houston, Dallas, San Antonio and Tyler are absent.

Google describes local ranking in terms of relevance, distance and prominence. A city page or service-area selection does not establish competitive Maps placement throughout Texas. Review actual eligible business operations before any profile expansion; do not create fictional locations. See [Google's local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

**Recommended action:** VP of Marketing and Customer Success should maintain accurate services/contact details, request honest reviews from eligible completed customers consistently, and measure answered calls and qualified inquiries. The profile website link is an untagged homepage; confirm the existing booking link and analytics behavior before adding consistent attribution tags. No profile edits or review messages were sent.

## 6. Business Impact

**What is supported:** Google sends relatively little traffic to the principal customer city pages; some recorded traffic serves recruitment; Maps interaction volume is small; two Tyler bookings did not become completed paid work; attribution is insufficient to rank markets by genuine organic profit.

**What is not established:** that competitors refuse Tyler, that all bookings originate there, that every canceled job was caused by unavailable Easers, that pricing is too high, that a new app would increase acquisition, or that a particular SEO change will guarantee consistent bookings. Recorded completed work includes Lubbock, San Antonio and Houston/Spring.

Customers risk uncertainty around timing and contact; Easers risk accepting mismatched scope or travel; the owner risks spending time on visibility that cannot be fulfilled. No wrong payout or new legal defect was established here. Accurate service claims and permissioned customer proof remain required.

The **CEO and VP of Marketing** should concentrate effort where existing visibility can become real jobs. **Marketplace Operations and VP of Supply** should ensure coverage means a capable person committed to an appointment. **Head of Data & AI and VP of Finance** should judge success by genuine completed, paid work and contribution after actual costs. **Head of Partnerships** should develop relevant referral relationships that can also earn legitimate local visibility.

## 10. Launch Recommendation

Continue accepting work that can be accurately scoped, staffed and fulfilled through the existing controls. This audit is not sufficient to certify the entire platform or justify changing payout mode. Do not infer statewide appointment capacity from the existence of city pages.

The next organic work should be an auditable set of measurement, customer-content and local-proof improvements, with selective indexing follow-up. Consistency must be demonstrated through actual completed jobs; neither publishing more city pages nor submitting every URL again establishes it.

**Chairman / Owner's Office ruling:** protect current customers first, measure genuine organic outcomes, and build local search visibility around work AssembleAtEase can reliably complete. Judge the program by completed, profitable jobs and customer trust.
