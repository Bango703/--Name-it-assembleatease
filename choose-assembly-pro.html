Buyer’s Guide

How to choose an assembly & home-setup pro.

A straight, no-spin guide to getting furniture, TVs, and whole rooms set up right — the questions to ask, the red flags to avoid, and an honest look at every option, including us.

Five questions to ask before you book.

Whoever you hire — us or someone else — these five answers tell you almost everything about how the job will go. If a service dodges any of them, that’s your answer.

1. Is the price clear before I book?

A trustworthy service gives you a real starting price up front. “We’ll figure it out when we get there” is how small jobs turn into big invoices.

2. Are the pros identity-verified?

Someone is coming into your home. You should know they were identity-checked and reviewed — not just whoever claimed the job first.

3. When do I pay?

The safest arrangement is paying after the work is done right. Be cautious with large upfront cash deposits before anyone has lifted a screwdriver.

4. What if something goes wrong?

Ask how they handle a wobbly build, a wrong part, or accidental damage. A real service has an answer before you ask.

5. Can I see real reviews?

Verified reviews from completed jobs beat a vague “5 stars” with no source. No reviews anywhere is a reason to keep looking.

Bonus: is scheduling realistic?

Same-day sounds great, but ask what’s actually available for your date and item — and whether they confirm quickly or leave you waiting.

Your options, honestly compared

Every way to get it done — the fair version.

There’s no single “best” answer; there’s a best answer for your job. Here’s where each option genuinely shines and where it doesn’t — including where we fit.

Do it yourself

Best for: simple, low-stakes items and a tight budget.

Trade-offs: your time and tools; complex beds, wardrobes, and wall-mounted TVs are where mistakes get expensive or unsafe.

Retailer / big-box add-on

Best for: a single item you’re buying new, assembled at delivery.

Trade-offs: usually limited to that one item, fixed scheduling windows, and quality that varies by whoever is subcontracted.

General handyman

Best for: a mix of odd jobs where assembly is one of several tasks.

Trade-offs: assembly may not be their specialty, and hourly pricing can be hard to predict before they arrive.

On-demand task apps

Best for: speed and a large pool of taskers.

Trade-offs: vetting and quality vary by tasker, pricing can surprise you, and you manage the relationship yourself.

Protect yourself

Red flags to walk away from.

Any one of these is a reason to pause. Two or more, and you should book someone else.

No clear price until they arrive
Large upfront cash deposits
No identity verification of the pro
No reviews you can actually find
Pressure to pay before the work is done
No plan for rework or damage
